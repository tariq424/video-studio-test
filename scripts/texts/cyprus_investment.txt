Cyprus is having a loud argument about who is buying the island, and why it matters.

According to Land Registry figures presented to the Cypriot parliament and reported by Cypriot and regional media, buyers with Israeli citizenship were involved in about 3,851 property transfers between 2021 and 2024, concentrated in the coastal districts of Larnaca, Limassol and Paphos. Cypriots in those districts say rents and house prices have climbed faster than local wages, and many blame foreign buyers of every nationality, including Russian, Chinese, Gulf and Israeli investors.

The bigger concern for critics is strategic, not residential. Israeli state-owned water company Mekorot is reported to run a large share of the island's desalination capacity through joint ventures with a Cypriot firm. Israel's NewMed Energy holds a stake in the Aphrodite gas field, according to its own stock-exchange filings. And Cyprus has bought Israeli-made air-defence systems.

Stefanos Stefanou, leader of the opposition party AKEL, compared the trend to land purchases in Mandate Palestine and warned that Cyprus could lose control of its own future. Cypriot MEP Fidias Panayiotou put it more bluntly: Israel is buying Cyprus, and the economy depends on that money.

Israel's embassy rejected the comparison as deeply concerning and warned that it fuels hostility toward Jews. Cyprus's government says it is monitoring the debate.

The real question for Cypriots is a familiar one in small open economies: how to welcome investment without handing strategic assets, water, energy and housing, to any single foreign power.
